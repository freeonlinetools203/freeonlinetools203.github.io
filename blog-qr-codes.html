What Is a QR Code and How Do Businesses Use It?

Have you ever scanned a square-shaped black-and-white pattern using your smartphone? If yes, you've probably used a QR code.

QR codes are everywhere today. You can find them on restaurant menus, product packaging, advertisements, business cards, and websites. They make it easy to access information without typing long website addresses or searching manually.

But what exactly is a QR code, and how can businesses use it? Let's explore everything you need to know.

What Is a QR Code?

QR stands for Quick Response. A QR code is a type of barcode that stores information in a pattern of black and white squares.

Unlike traditional barcodes, which usually store information in one direction, QR codes can store information both horizontally and vertically. A smartphone camera or QR code scanner can read the pattern and display the stored information.

A QR code can contain different types of information, including:

  • Website URLs
  • Contact information
  • Phone numbers
  • Email addresses
  • Wi-Fi network details
  • Text messages
  • Payment information

For example, a business can create a QR code that takes customers directly to its website.

How Does a QR Code Work?

Using a QR code is simple. First, someone creates a QR code containing specific information. That code can then be printed or shared digitally.

When another person scans it using a smartphone, the device reads the pattern and processes the stored information.

For example:

  1. Open your smartphone camera.
  2. Point it toward the QR code.
  3. Wait for a notification or link to appear.
  4. Tap the notification to open the information.

Some older phones may require a separate QR code scanner application.

How Do Businesses Use QR Codes?

Businesses use QR codes in different ways to make everyday activities easier.

1. Restaurant Digital Menus

Many restaurants use QR codes instead of printed menus. Customers scan the code at their table and view the menu on their smartphones. This makes it easier for restaurants to update prices, add new dishes, and change menu items.

2. Product Information

Companies can place QR codes on product packaging. Customers can scan them to learn more about ingredients, instructions, product details, or warranty information.

3. Website Promotion

Businesses can print QR codes on posters, flyers, and advertisements. When customers scan the code, they can visit the company's website without manually entering its address.

4. Business Cards

QR codes can make traditional business cards more useful. A QR code can direct people to a digital contact card containing a person's name, phone number, email address, and website.

5. Payments

Some businesses use QR codes to make digital payments easier. Customers scan a payment QR code using a supported banking or payment application and follow the instructions to complete the transaction. Always verify payment details before confirming a transaction.

Static vs. Dynamic QR Codes

There are two common types of QR codes.

  • Static QR Codes: These contain information directly in the code. Once created, the stored information generally cannot be changed without generating a new QR code.
  • Dynamic QR Codes: These usually redirect through a managed link, allowing the destination to be changed without replacing the printed code. Some services also provide scan analytics.

Dynamic QR code services may require a paid subscription.
