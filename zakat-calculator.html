1. What Is Zakat in Islam?

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam and an important obligation for eligible Muslims. It is a prescribed form of giving that helps people in need and encourages fairness, generosity, and responsibility in the use of wealth.

For eligible wealth, the commonly applied Zakat rate is 2.5%. However, Zakat is not calculated on every item a person owns. It generally applies to qualifying wealth, such as cash, savings, gold, silver, investments, and certain business assets, when the relevant conditions are met.

Our free online Zakat calculator helps you organize your assets and liabilities, check your net wealth against the silver Nisab value you enter, and estimate the Zakat amount at 2.5%.

Whether you are calculating Zakat on savings, gold, investments, or business assets, understanding the basic calculation can make the process easier.

2. What Is Nisab and How Does It Work?

Nisab is the minimum wealth threshold that determines whether a person may become liable to pay Zakat. The traditional Nisab measures are based on gold or silver.

The commonly used reference weights are:

  • Gold Nisab: Approximately 87.48 grams of gold.
  • Silver Nisab: Approximately 612.36 grams of silver.

The cash value of Nisab changes with the market prices of gold and silver. Therefore, a Nisab amount entered for one year may not be suitable for another year.

Gold Nisab or Silver Nisab: Which Should You Use?

Islamic scholars and schools of thought may differ on which Nisab measure should be used in particular circumstances. The silver standard is commonly used in many Zakat calculations, while other guidance may use the gold standard.

Our calculator includes a Silver Value (Nisab Reference) field. Enter the current market value of approximately 612.36 grams of silver in the same currency used for your other amounts. The calculator uses this entered amount only as the Nisab threshold, not as part of your total assets.

The silver value should be updated according to the current market price when you calculate Zakat. Our calculator does not automatically fetch live silver prices.

For personal religious guidance, follow a qualified scholar or the school of thought you normally follow.

3. How to Calculate Zakat (2.5%)

To calculate Zakat, first work out the total value of your eligible assets. Then subtract the liabilities that are deductible according to the guidance you follow. This gives you your net wealth for the calculation.

Basic Zakat Calculation Formula

Step 1: Total Assets = Add the values of your eligible assets.

Step 2: Net Wealth = Total Assets − Deductible Liabilities.

Step 3: Check whether Net Wealth meets or exceeds Nisab.

Step 4: Zakat = Net Wealth × 2.5%.

For example, if your eligible net wealth is 500,000 and it meets the applicable Nisab threshold, the basic 2.5% calculation would be:

500,000 × 0.025 = 12,500

This is an illustrative calculation. Your actual Zakat assessment depends on your eligible assets, deductible liabilities, Nisab, and the relevant conditions for your circumstances.

4. Which Assets Should You Include in Zakat?

When using an online Zakat calculator, it is important to enter the correct values for the assets that may be subject to Zakat. Common categories include cash, savings, gold, silver, investments, and business stock held for sale.

Cash in Hand

Include the cash you own at home or carry with you on your Zakat calculation date.

Cash in Bank Accounts

Include eligible balances in your current, savings, and other bank accounts. Add the relevant balances together before entering the amount.

Gold and Silver

Enter the current market value of the gold that is subject to Zakat under the guidance you follow. Rules about personal-use jewellery can differ between Islamic schools of thought. If you own Zakatable silver, include its value in Other Savings, without counting it twice.

Investments

Include the applicable value of investments, shares, and other qualifying financial assets. The treatment of different investment types can vary depending on their nature and purpose.

Business Assets

Business stock and goods held for resale may be included in Zakat calculations. Personal-use equipment and other business property may be treated differently from goods purchased for sale.

Other Savings

Include other qualifying savings or money that should be considered in your Zakat assessment. This may also include the eligible value of silver you own. Avoid counting the same money or asset twice in different categories.

Important: Our calculator's Silver Value (Nisab Reference) field is used only to check the Nisab threshold. Do not enter this reference amount as your own silver assets. If you own silver, enter its eligible value separately under Other Savings.

5. How to Calculate Zakat on Gold

Zakat on gold is a common question, especially for people who own gold jewellery, coins, or bars. The calculation generally requires knowing the weight, purity, and current market value of the gold that is subject to Zakat.

Steps to Calculate Zakat on Gold

  1. Find the weight of the gold you own.
  2. Check its purity or karat, such as 24K, 22K, or 18K.
  3. Find the current market value applicable to that gold.
  4. Calculate the total value of the gold that is subject to Zakat.
  5. Enter that total market value in the Gold Value (Market Price) field of our calculator.

Example of Gold Zakat Calculation

Suppose the eligible value of your gold is 400,000. If your total net Zakatable wealth meets the Nisab requirements, the 2.5% calculation on that gold value alone would be:

400,000 × 0.025 = 10,000

This example shows the calculation on the gold value only. If you also own cash, savings, investments, or other Zakatable assets, those may need to be considered together rather than calculating each category as a separate obligation.

Does Zakat Apply to Gold Jewellery?

Scholars differ on the Zakat treatment of gold jewellery kept for personal use. For example, the Hanafi school generally includes gold and silver jewellery, while other schools may exempt customary personal-use jewellery under certain conditions. Gold held for trade is treated differently from personal-use items.

If you are unsure whether your jewellery is subject to Zakat, consult a qualified Islamic scholar before making your calculation.

6. Debts and Liabilities in Zakat Calculation

Liabilities can affect your net wealth when calculating Zakat. Our calculator provides two fields for entering debts and other liabilities.

Debts (Total Outstanding)

Other Liabilities

Enter other qualifying financial obligations that may be deducted from your assets. The treatment of future bills, long-term loans, instalments, and other commitments can differ.

Important: Do not automatically subtract every future expense or the full balance of every long-term loan. Deductible liabilities depend on the applicable Islamic guidance and the nature of the debt.
