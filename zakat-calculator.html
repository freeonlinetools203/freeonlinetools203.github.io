2. Nisab for Zakat in Pakistan (2026)

Nisab is the minimum wealth threshold used to determine whether a person may be liable to pay Zakat. The commonly cited Nisab standards are based on the value of gold or silver. Their equivalent value in Pakistani rupees changes with market prices.

Nisab TypeWeightTola
Gold Nisab87.48 grams7.5 tola
Silver Nisab612.36 grams52.5 tola

The cash value of Nisab changes with market prices, especially gold and silver prices. Before calculating Zakat, check the current gold or silver rate in Pakistan.

How to Calculate Nisab Value in PKR

To calculate the Nisab value in Pakistani rupees, multiply the current price per gram by the standard Nisab weight.

  • Gold Nisab: Current gold price per gram × 87.48
  • Silver Nisab: Current silver price per gram × 612.36

For example, if gold costs Rs 35,000 per gram, the gold Nisab value would be Rs 3,061,800. If silver costs Rs 415 per gram, the silver Nisab value would be approximately Rs 254,129. These are example prices, not current market rates.

Many people use the silver Nisab because its value is lower than the gold Nisab. If you are unsure which Nisab to follow, ask a trusted Islamic scholar.

Our calculator includes a Silver Value (Nisab Reference) field. Enter the current value of 612.36 grams of silver in PKR to compare your net wealth with the Nisab threshold.

Important: The Silver Nisab Reference is used only to check the Nisab threshold. It is not added to your total assets. If you own silver, enter its value separately under Other Savings.

3. How to Calculate Zakat in PKR (2.5%)

You can understand the basic Zakat calculation in four simple steps:

Step 1: Add your eligible assets, such as cash, bank savings, gold, silver, investments, and business stock.

Step 2: Subtract the debts and liabilities that can be deducted.

Step 3: Compare your remaining wealth with the Nisab value.

Step 4: If Zakat is due, calculate 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth.

For example, if your net zakatable wealth is Rs 1,000,000, your Zakat at 2.5% would be:

Rs 1,000,000 × 0.025 = Rs 25,000

This is a simple example. Your actual Zakat amount depends on your eligible assets, deductible debts, Nisab value, and personal circumstances.

Note: The conditions for Zakat include the passage of one full lunar year (hawl) in many common situations. The rules about when the year starts or restarts can vary. If your wealth falls below Nisab during the year, ask a trusted Islamic scholar how this affects your Zakat date.

4. Which Assets Are Zakatable in Pakistan?

Some types of wealth may be included when calculating Zakat, while personal-use belongings are generally treated differently. Here are common examples:

Cash in Hand

Include the cash you have at home, in your wallet, or with you on your Zakat calculation date.

Bank Accounts

Include eligible balances in your current, savings, and other bank accounts. Avoid counting the same money more than once.

Gold and Silver

Gold and silver may be included in Zakat calculations. This can include jewellery, coins, and bars. Rules about jewellery worn for personal use can differ between Islamic schools of thought.

Investments and Shares

Shares, mutual funds, savings certificates, and other investments may need to be included. The calculation can depend on the type of investment and whether it is held for saving or business purposes.

Business Assets

Goods held for sale, business stock, inventory, and raw materials may be included in business Zakat. Equipment used to run the business, such as computers and delivery vehicles, is generally treated differently from goods held for sale.

Money Loaned Out (Receivables)

If someone owes you money, its treatment may depend on whether you expect to receive it and the type of debt. Check the relevant Zakat guidance before including it.

Other Savings

Other savings, such as foreign currency and qualifying silver you own, may also need to be considered. The treatment of prize bonds and some financial products can vary, so check the guidance you follow.

Personal belongings: Your primary home, personal car, household furniture, clothing, and tools used for your work are generally not included in Zakat calculations when they are for personal use rather than sale.

5. How to Calculate Zakat on Gold in Pakistan

Gold is a common form of savings in Pakistan. If you own gold jewellery, coins, or bars, you may need to include their value when calculating Zakat. The rules for personal-use jewellery can differ between Islamic schools of thought.

Steps to Calculate Zakat on Gold

  1. Weigh your gold in grams or tola. (1 tola is approximately 11.66 grams.)
  2. Check the gold purity, such as 24K, 22K, 21K, or 18K.
  3. Find the current gold price in Pakistan for that purity.
  4. Multiply the gold weight by the applicable price to estimate its value.
  5. Enter the total value in the Gold Value field of our calculator.

Example of Gold Zakat Calculation

Suppose you own 50 grams of 22K gold. If the example price is Rs 32,000 per gram, the estimated value of your gold would be:

50 × Rs 32,000 = Rs 1,600,000

If this gold is included in your zakatable wealth and Zakat is due, 2.5% of its example value would be:

Rs 1,600,000 × 0.025 = Rs 40,000

This is only an example of the calculation on gold. Your final Zakat amount should be calculated using all your eligible assets and deductible liabilities together.

Does Zakat Apply to Gold Jewellery?

The rules for gold jewellery can differ between Islamic schools of thought. According to the Hanafi school, gold and silver jewellery are generally included in Zakat, even when used personally. Some other schools have different rules for jewellery kept for personal use. If you are unsure, ask a trusted Islamic scholar.

7. Zakat on Cash and Bank Accounts

Cash and bank balances are common items to consider when organizing your Zakat calculation. Depending on the applicable rules, these may include:

  • Cash kept at home or in your wallet
  • Current account balances
  • Savings account balances
  • Eligible fixed deposit amounts
  • Foreign currency converted into PKR
  • Money held in digital wallets, such as Easypaisa or JazzCash, if it is part of your savings

Do not count the same money twice. For example, if you have already entered your bank balance, do not add that same amount again under Cash in Hand.

If you have a loan or credit card balance, only deduct the amount that is eligible for deduction under the Zakat rules you follow. Long-term loans may be treated differently, so check with a trusted Islamic scholar if you are unsure.

8. Zakat on Investments, Shares and Mutual Funds

Investments in Pakistan may include shares, mutual funds, National Savings schemes, and prize bonds. How these investments are treated in Zakat calculations can depend on the type of investment and the purpose for which you hold it.

Shares

Some scholars recommend calculating Zakat using the current market value of shares, while others consider the company's zakatable assets. The method may also depend on whether you hold shares for trading or long-term investment.

Mutual Funds

The Zakat treatment of mutual funds may depend on the assets held by the fund and the purpose of your investment. Check the fund information and follow the guidance of a trusted Islamic scholar.

National Savings

National Savings certificates and accounts may need to be included in your Zakat calculation. The amount to consider can depend on the type of scheme and the applicable Zakat rules.

Prize Bonds

The Zakat treatment of prize bonds can differ among scholars. If you own prize bonds, ask a trusted Islamic scholar whether and how their value should be included.

Enter the value of your eligible investments in the Investments field of our calculator.

9. Zakat on Business Assets and Stock

If you run a business in Pakistan, certain business assets may be included in your Zakat calculation. These commonly include goods held for sale and eligible money owed by customers. Equipment used to operate the business is generally treated differently.

What to Include

  • Goods held for sale (inventory)
  • Raw materials used to make products for sale
  • Finished goods ready for sale
  • Eligible money owed by customers (receivables)
  • Business cash held in hand or in bank accounts

What Not to Include

  • Shop furniture and fixtures used in the business
  • Delivery vehicles used for business work
  • Computers and office equipment used to run the business
  • Tools and equipment used for your trade

Enter the total value of your eligible business assets in the Business Assets field of our calculator.

10. Debts and Liabilities in Zakat Calculation

Some debts and unpaid amounts may be deducted before calculating Zakat. Our calculator provides two fields for entering these amounts: Debts (Total Outstanding) and Other Liabilities.

Examples of Liabilities to Check

  • Debts that you need to repay
  • Utility bills or rent that are already due
  • Employee wages that are due for payment
  • Credit card payments that are currently due

Other Payments and Loans

  • Long-term loans, such as a home loan, may not be fully deductible. The treatment of upcoming instalments can depend on the guidance you follow.
  • Future bills that are not yet due are not automatically deductible.
  • Do not automatically include future interest payments as deductible liabilities.

Important: Not every expense or future payment can be subtracted from your wealth. The rules for deductible debts can vary. If you are unsure which amounts to enter, consult a trusted Islamic scholar.
