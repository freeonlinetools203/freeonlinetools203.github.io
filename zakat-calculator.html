1. What Is Zakat?

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam. It is a compulsory act of worship for every eligible Muslim who owns wealth above a certain minimum threshold. Zakat purifies your wealth and helps those in need.

In Pakistan, Zakat is calculated at 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth held for one full lunar year (hawl). The wealth must meet or exceed the Nisab threshold.

2. Nisab for Zakat in Pakistan (2026)

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must own before Zakat becomes obligatory. There are two standards: gold Nisab and silver Nisab.

Nisab TypeWeightTola
Gold Nisab87.48 grams7.5 tola
Silver Nisab612.36 grams52.5 tola

The cash value of Nisab changes every day because gold and silver prices change. In Pakistan, you should check the current rate per gram or per tola before calculating your Zakat.

How to Check Nisab Value in PKR

To find your Nisab value in rupees, multiply the current gold or silver rate per gram by the Nisab weight.

  • Gold Nisab in PKR = Current gold rate per gram × 87.48
  • Silver Nisab in PKR = Current silver rate per gram × 612.36

For example, if gold is Rs 35,000 per gram, the gold Nisab would be approximately Rs 3,061,800. If silver is Rs 415 per gram, the silver Nisab would be approximately Rs 254,129.

Most scholars in Pakistan recommend using the silver Nisab because it is lower and more beneficial for the poor. Our calculator has a dedicated Silver Value (Nisab Reference) field where you enter the current market value of 612.36 grams of silver in PKR.

Important: The silver value you enter is used only as the Nisab threshold. It is not added to your total assets. Enter your own silver separately under Other Savings if you own any.

3. How to Calculate Zakat in PKR (2.5%)

The Zakat calculation in Pakistan follows a simple four-step formula.

Step 1: Add all your zakatable assets (cash, bank, gold, silver, investments, business stock, receivables).

Step 2: Subtract deductible liabilities (debts, immediate bills, wages due).

Step 3: Compare your net wealth with the Nisab threshold.

Step 4: If net wealth is equal to or above Nisab, Zakat = Net Wealth × 2.5%.

Example: If your net zakatable wealth is Rs 1,000,000 and it is above the Nisab, your Zakat would be:

1,000,000 × 0.025 = Rs 25,000

This is a simple example. Your actual Zakat depends on your assets, deductible liabilities, Nisab, and personal circumstances.

Note: Zakat is due only if you have held wealth equal to or above Nisab for one full lunar year. If your wealth dropped below Nisab at any point during the year, the hawl cycle may restart.

4. Which Assets Are Zakatable in Pakistan?

Not everything you own is subject to Zakat. Here are the common zakatable assets in Pakistan:

Cash in Hand

All cash you have at home, in your wallet, or with you on your Zakat calculation date.

Bank Accounts

Money in current, savings, and fixed deposit accounts. Include all balances, including the amounts saved for bills or rent.

Gold and Silver

Gold jewellery, coins, bars, and silver items. Rules about personal-use jewellery differ between schools of thought. In Pakistan, most Hanafi scholars include gold and silver jewellery in Zakat.

Investments and Shares

Shares, mutual funds, savings certificates, national savings, and other financial investments. The treatment may vary depending on the type of investment.

Business Assets

Business stock, inventory, raw materials, and goods held for sale. Personal-use business equipment (like a computer or delivery bike) is usually not zakatable.

Money Loaned Out (Receivables)

Money you have lent to someone and expect to get back. If you are confident of receiving it, include it in your assets.

Other Savings

Other qualifying savings, including foreign currency, prize bonds (according to some scholars), and any zakatable silver you own.

Not zakatable: Your primary home, personal car, household furniture, clothing, and tools of trade. These are considered basic needs and are generally exempt from Zakat.

5. How to Calculate Zakat on Gold in Pakistan

Zakat on gold is the most common question in Pakistan. Gold is a popular savings asset, and most families own gold jewellery, coins, or bars.

Steps to Calculate Zakat on Gold

  1. Weigh your gold in grams or tola. (1 tola = 11.66 grams)
  2. Check the purity: 24K, 22K, 21K, or 18K. Pure gold is 24K.
  3. Find the current gold rate per gram in Pakistan for your purity level.
  4. Calculate the total value: weight × current rate.
  5. Enter this total value in the Gold Value field of our calculator.

Example of Gold Zakat Calculation

Suppose you own 50 grams of 22K gold. If the current rate for 22K gold is Rs 32,000 per gram, the total value is:

50 × 32,000 = Rs 1,600,000

If your total net zakatable wealth meets the Nisab, the Zakat on this gold alone would be:

1,600,000 × 0.025 = Rs 40,000

Remember to add your other assets (cash, bank, investments) before calculating the final Zakat amount.

Does Zakat Apply to Gold Jewellery?

In Pakistan, the Hanafi school of thought generally includes gold and silver jewellery in Zakat, even if it is kept for personal use. Other schools may exempt customary personal-use jewellery under certain conditions. If you are unsure, consult a qualified scholar.

6. Zakat on Silver in Pakistan

Silver is also a zakatable asset. The silver Nisab is 612.36 grams (52.5 tola). If you own silver equal to or above this weight, Zakat applies at 2.5%.

Our calculator uses the current market value of 612.36 grams of silver as the Nisab reference. Enter that value in the Silver Value field. If you own silver yourself, add its value under Other Savings.

Silver is often cheaper than gold, which is why many scholars prefer the silver Nisab. It makes Zakat obligatory for more people and benefits more needy Muslims.

7. Zakat on Cash and Bank Accounts

Cash and bank balances are the most common zakatable assets. Here is what to include:

  • Cash in hand at home or in your wallet
  • Current account balances
  • Savings account balances
  • Fixed deposit balances
  • Foreign currency converted to PKR
  • Money in digital wallets (like Easypaisa, JazzCash) if held as savings

Do not double-count the same money. If you have already included a bank balance, do not add it again under cash in hand.

If you have a loan or credit card debt, you may deduct the amount due immediately. Long-term loans may be treated differently depending on the guidance you follow.

8. Zakat on Investments, Shares and Mutual Funds

Investments in Pakistan can include shares, mutual funds, national savings certificates, and prize bonds. The Zakat treatment depends on the type of investment.

Shares

For shares, some scholars say to calculate Zakat on the market value of the shares. Others recommend calculating Zakat on the company's zakatable assets per share. Follow the guidance you trust.

Mutual Funds

For mutual funds, check the fund's underlying assets. If the fund invests in zakatable assets, include the current value in your Zakat calculation.

National Savings

Savings certificates and accounts are generally zakatable. Include the current balance or maturity value, depending on the scheme.

Prize Bonds

Scholars differ on prize bonds. Some include the face value as zakatable wealth. Consult a scholar for your specific situation.

Enter the total value of your qualifying investments in the Investments field of our calculator.

9. Zakat on Business Assets and Stock

If you run a business in Pakistan, Zakat applies to your business stock and receivables, not to your personal-use business equipment.

What to Include

  • Goods held for sale (inventory)
  • Raw materials for production
  • Finished goods ready for sale
  • Money owed to you by customers (receivables)
  • Business cash in hand and bank

What Not to Include

  • Shop furniture and fixtures
  • Delivery vehicles
  • Computers and office equipment
  • Tools used for your trade

Enter the total value of your zakatable business assets in the Business Assets field.

10. Debts and Liabilities in Zakat Calculation

You can subtract certain liabilities from your assets before calculating Zakat. Our calculator has two fields: Debts (Total Outstanding) and Other Liabilities.

What to Deduct

  • Money you owe to others and must repay soon
  • Immediate bills (utility bills, rent due now)
  • Employee wages due
  • Credit card balances due immediately

What Not to Deduct Automatically

  • Long-term loans (like a 20-year home loan) — deduct only the current year's instalments
  • Future bills not yet due
  • Interest-based amounts (avoid including interest in your calculation)

Important: Do not subtract every future expense. Deductible liabilities depend on the Islamic guidance you follow. When in doubt, consult a scholar.

14. Pro Tips and Common Mistakes

Pro Tips

  • Pick a fixed date: Choose a date in Ramadan or any fixed Islamic date to calculate Zakat every year. This makes tracking easier.
  • Use the silver Nisab: Many scholars prefer it because it is lower and helps more people.
  • Update gold and silver rates: Prices change daily. Always use the current rate on your calculation date.
  • Include all bank accounts: Don't forget savings accounts, fixed deposits, and digital wallets.
  • Check receivables: Money lent to others may be zakatable if you expect to receive it back.
  • Keep records: Save your calculation each year for reference and consistency.

Common Mistakes

  • Counting the Nisab reference as an asset: The silver value you enter is only a threshold, not your wealth.
  • Forgetting gold jewellery: In Pakistan, most scholars include jewellery in Zakat.
  • Deducting full long-term loans: Only deduct the amount due immediately, not the entire loan.
  • Using old gold rates: Always check today's rate before calculating.
  • Double-counting assets: Do not enter the same money under multiple categories.
  • Ignoring the hawl: Zakat is due only after holding wealth above Nisab for one lunar year.
