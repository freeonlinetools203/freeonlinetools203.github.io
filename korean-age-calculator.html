🇰🇷 1. What is Korean Age?

Korean age (also called "Korean age system" or "East Asian age reckoning") is a unique age calculation method used in South Korea. In this system:

  • At birth: A person is considered 1 year old
  • On New Year's Day (January 1): Everyone's age increases by 1
  • This means Koreans are usually 1-2 years older than their international age

📌 Did You Know? The Korean age system is based on traditional East Asian culture where the time spent in the womb is counted as 1 year.

📐 2. How to Calculate Korean Age

The formula for calculating Korean age is simple:

Korean Age = Current Year – Birth Year + 1

Example:

  • Birth Year: 1990
  • Current Year: 2026
  • Korean Age = 2026 - 1990 + 1 = 37
  • International Age = 2026 - 1990 = 36 (or less depending on birthday)

📚 6. Why Does Korea Use This System?

The Korean age system has its roots in traditional East Asian culture. The concept is that a baby's life begins in the womb, so they are already 1 year old at birth. Additionally, everyone ages together on New Year's Day, emphasizing collective aging rather than individual birthdays.

This system is still widely used in:

  • Social interactions (determining who is older/younger)
  • Cultural traditions and events
  • School entrance age calculations
  • Military service eligibility

📝 7. Did Korea Change Their Age System?

Yes! In June 2023, South Korea officially adopted the international age system for legal and official documents. This means that for:

  • Legal documents and contracts
  • Official government records
  • Medical and legal purposes
  • International age is now the official standard

However, many Koreans still use Korean age informally in daily life, social situations, and cultural contexts.
